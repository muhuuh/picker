## What LPKF Actually Does

LPKF Laser & Electronics SE designs and sells laser-based precision manufacturing systems. It operates across Development, Electronics, Welding, and Solar. That matters because the company is not a clean semiconductor-equipment pure play today. Its reported results still include PCB prototyping, laser welding, and solar equipment, and those businesses can drag the numbers even if LIDE is strategically valuable.

The important product for this thesis is LIDE, or Laser Induced Deep Etching. LIDE is used to structure thin glass substrates with high precision. In investor terms, the claim is that LIDE may enable defect-controlled, high-aspect-ratio through-glass vias and other glass features needed for denser, lower-loss, thermally better advanced packages.

Why this matters:

- AI/HPC systems need denser interconnects and better thermal/electrical behavior.
- Glass substrates are one possible answer to packaging bottlenecks.
- If glass substrates and TGVs scale, process equipment becomes a bottleneck.
- LPKF may sit at that bottleneck through LIDE rather than competing directly in AI chips.

The moat claim is not "LPKF is large." It is the opposite: a small specialist may have a hard-to-replace process if LIDE is qualified by important customers. The risk is that this moat is still not proven by public high-volume order evidence.

Market context from the Grok web deep dive:

- Advanced semiconductor packaging and thin-glass processing are expected to benefit from AI/HPC demand through 2027.
- Solar and general electronics remain cyclical and face Chinese competitive pressure.
- Competitors include Korean and Chinese laser-equipment makers plus other German equipment players.
- LPKF's potential advantages are LIDE IP, process know-how, foundry/service capability, and a global service footprint.
- Its disadvantages are smaller scale, cost structure versus Asian rivals, and reliance on customers moving from qualification to production.

This creates a very specific thesis. LPKF does not need to win broad semiconductor capex. It needs glass-packaging adoption to pull LIDE systems into production capacity.

## Why The Setup Changed

The old read was "German industrial laser company with cyclical exposure." The new read is "possible AI packaging bottleneck with weak current earnings but improving order signals."

The setup changed in four ways.

First, Q1 2026 gave a split signal. Revenue and EBIT were bad, but order intake improved. That is why the report should not be simply bullish or bearish. Revenue of EUR 17.1M and adjusted EBIT of EUR -5.7M show current weakness. Order intake of EUR 24.1M and book-to-bill of 1.4 show potential stabilization.

Second, management's language moved the LIDE story closer to an evidence gate. The Q1 release and extracted content say Advanced Semiconductor Packaging is making good progress and that LPKF is in concrete discussions with several customers for initial production equipment. That is not a signed order, but it is better than pure social speculation.

Third, the North Star program gives the company a cost bridge. The company says the program is progressing and should support a medium-term target of sustainable double-digit EBIT margin by 2028. That matters because LPKF may need time for LIDE production revenue to arrive. If North Star lowers the cost base, the company has more room to wait.

Fourth, X and specialist accounts accelerated the glass/TGV narrative. The raw Grok/X artifact says the last two weeks saw the community coalesce around LPKF as the clearest pure-play TGV equipment supplier, with social discussion citing strong YTD price performance, production-order speculation, and possible customer roadmaps.

What changed is not only "price went up." What changed is the decision question:

| Old question | New question |
| --- | --- |
| Can LPKF recover from weak solar/electronics cycles? | Can LIDE convert from promising process technology into production equipment orders? |

The setup is not clean yet. It is attractive because the evidence gate is concrete: either customer orders appear, or they do not.

Main bear points:

- Q1 revenue fell sharply year over year, and EBIT remained meaningfully negative.
- Solar weakness was the main revenue drag, and that weakness may persist.
- LIDE order discussions are not the same as production orders.
- The stock appears to have moved before confirmed series-production evidence.

The hardest bear version is not "LIDE is hype." It is "LIDE is promising but not enough, not soon enough, or not uniquely LPKF enough."
