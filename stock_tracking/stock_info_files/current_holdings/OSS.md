## Current Opinion

- Summary: Current holding with improving continuing-operations momentum after the Bressner divestiture. The thesis is rugged edge AI for defense/industrial/medical use cases, not yet broad humanoid demand.
- Confidence: medium
- Current decision: hold for research review
- What would change our mind: weak continuing-operations growth, book-to-bill deterioration, margin normalization worse than expected, defense-budget delays, or failure to convert edge-AI prototypes into production programs.

## Thesis

- Why this company is interesting: OSS sells rugged deployable compute for edge AI, autonomy, sensor processing, defense, medical imaging, and harsh environments where cloud-only AI is not sufficient.
- What must be true: continuing operations must sustain growth, bookings must convert into revenue, and rugged edge-AI programs must scale beyond prototypes.
- Main upside drivers: Q1 continuing-ops growth, book-to-bill around 1.8x, defense/medical demand, P-8A storage, enhanced-vision compute prototypes, and higher-margin post-Bressner business mix.
- Main downside risks: microcap liquidity, customer concentration, budget timing, supply-chain/memory costs, and gross-margin normalization after strong Q1 mix.

## Follow-up Research Notes

### 2026-05-17 User Follow-up Research

- Apparent revenue decline is primarily from the Bressner subsidiary sale/discontinued operations, not proof that the core business is shrinking.
- Continuing operations are growing: fresh sources point to Q1 2026 revenue around $8.1M, up about 55% YoY, gross margin around 51.6%, positive adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow around $4.0M, and book-to-bill around 1.8x.
- FY2026 guidance points to 20-25% continuing-operations revenue growth. Future reports should compare continuing operations, not total revenue polluted by the discontinued Bressner base.
- OSS's edge/on-device AI positioning is tangible for rugged deployable high-performance compute, defense sensor processing, P-8A Poseidon data storage, enhanced-vision compute prototypes for combat vehicles, and medical imaging OEM systems.
- Near-term thesis is defense/industrial/medical rugged edge AI. Humanoid/autonomous robotics upside is optionality, not currently the proven revenue base.
